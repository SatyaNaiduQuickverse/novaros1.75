The red arm pair is the FRONT, and the red arrow is the nose — where the camera looks. Yaw is forced to zero in both, because it is not observable from gravity and the ESP32 has no magnetometer; only roll and pitch mean anything here. If you tip the nose up and a drawing tips its nose down, that is a sign error, and it will be obvious long before the numbers give it away.

Sign check — you label the truth

Hold the airframe in a position, then press its button. Both sensors are recorded and checked against what that position REQUIRES. Nothing here is inferred — you are the ground truth, which is the point: every sign error so far came from something inferring rather than being told.

Positions captured

Hold each position about a second. Order and precision do not matter — anywhere within roughly 30° counts. The tile you are currently in lights blue, then turns green once captured.

1Accelerometer offset & scale

90 seconds. Tumble the airframe so gravity visits every face. This finds the per-axis zero error that tilts which way the sensor believes is down — measured at 0.154 g on this part, worth 8.8°.

2Axis map, against the flight controller

60 seconds. Tumble it again, exactly the same way. The flight controller is a second IMU on the same rigid airframe, so comparing the two recovers how the ESP32 is mounted — which body axis is which, and the signs. No named poses are required: the FC supplies ground truth, so you do not have to hit any particular angle.

  • Nose up — nose toward the sky (pitch stick back)
  • Nose down — nose toward the ground (pitch stick forward)
  • Roll right / left — that side down
  • Level / Inverted — upright, then upside down

3Verify — independently

Nothing above can certify itself: the attitude is derived from the very sensor being calibrated. These two checks use a second, independent sensor and your own hands.

Agreeing at rest proves only the static calibration. Press start, then tilt it through 40° or so in both axes — a wrong sign downstream still looks perfect at zero tilt and diverges the moment the airframe moves.

3b — does the correction oppose the tilt

Runs the real compiled module against the live IMU. Its output goes to a stand-in, so nothing reaches the flight controller — no RC is streamed and it cannot arm. Tilt the airframe and watch which way it commands.
